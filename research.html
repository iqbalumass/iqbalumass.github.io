Research

Secure, Robust, and Trustworthy Multimodal Intelligence

My research focuses on making AI systems more reliable when they learn from images, language, and sensor data. I study how adversarial attacks, unreliable retrieved information, and incomplete observations affect these systems, and how we can develop methods that remain useful under those conditions.

My work brings together computer vision, adversarial machine learning, and multimodal reasoning. A central question connects these directions: how can an AI system use the available evidence to make a trustworthy decision when part of that evidence is missing or misleading?

Vision-Language Model Security

I investigate vulnerabilities in vision-language models, including backdoor attacks and data poisoning. My work on multimodal contrastive learning studies how to identify potential triggers and affected samples, then use targeted fine-tuning to reduce backdoor effects. I also examine the broader attack and defense landscape to identify limitations in current approaches and directions for future research.

Related work: Backdoor defense for multimodal contrastive learning · Survey of adversarial attacks and defenses on vision-language models

Reliable Retrieval-Augmented Image Generation

Retrieved images can help a generative model follow a user’s request, but poisoned examples can also steer the output away from the intended content. My work on robust vision RAG explores semantic consistency between the prompt and retrieved examples, with the goal of reducing the influence of misleading retrievals while preserving the user’s intent. This work considers visual quality, prompt alignment, and resistance to poisoning as distinct evaluation goals.

Multimodal Perception and Sensing

I explore how camera and radar observations can provide complementary information about a scene. This direction studies the alignment of sensor representations with visual and language features, supporting scene understanding when a single modality offers an incomplete view. My broader goal is to connect multimodal perception with interpretable reasoning about the surrounding environment.

Cooperative Reasoning for Autonomous Systems

My ongoing research investigates how connected vehicles and roadside units can combine observations to reason about hidden road hazards. I study occlusion-aware reasoning that uses cooperative detections, object positions, and motion information to assess potential conflicts and generate interpretable safety recommendations. This direction also motivates questions about the reliability of cooperative reasoning when individual perception systems make errors.
