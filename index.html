About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate (all but dissertation) at the University of Massachusetts Dartmouth, studying Engineering and Applied Science with a concentration in Computer Science and Information Systems. My research focuses on secure and trustworthy multimodal intelligence: understanding how AI systems fail when their information is manipulated or incomplete, and developing ways to detect and correct those failures.

My work brings together computer vision, vision-language models, adversarial machine learning, and cooperative reasoning for connected vehicles. As a graduate research assistant, I have contributed to NSF-funded research on robust multimodal models and adversarial attacks and defenses.

I bring nearly eight years of industry experience to my research and teaching. Before beginning my doctoral studies, I worked at Spectrum Engineering Consortium and Samsung R&D Institute Bangladesh, progressing from programming and software engineering to technical leadership.

I am pursuing a faculty career that combines student-centered teaching, research, and mentoring. I want students to understand why an algorithm works, connect computational ideas to practical problems, and develop the confidence to investigate questions of their own.
